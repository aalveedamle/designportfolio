yulu — information architecture

Every state the rider app can be in between opening it and finishing a ride, what each one holds, and where it goes when things fail. Built from the same readiness rule the product runs on: required = max(trip × 1.5, 2 km).

5 phases · 12 steps · 12 branch states · 4 cross-cutting layers · 5 persistent surfaces

00Three decisions the whole structure rests on

An IA is mostly the consequences of a few early choices. These are the three that produced everything below — and the third one is the reason this document exists at all.

DECISION 01

Destination is the root of the ride funnel — not the map.

Readiness is trip-dependent, so a bike that clears 3 km does not clear 9 km. That makes the map a result, not a place. If the map were the root, the first screen could only promise a 2 km floor — a weaker guarantee than the product is built to make.

DECISION 02

The funnel is modal, not navigable.

No tab bar once a destination is set. From A7 onward the rider is inside a task holding a payment authorisation and possibly a reserved bike; letting her wander into Account mid-funnel creates states nobody designed. Persistent surfaces hang off A5/A6 only.

DECISION 03

Ride state lives on the server. The app is a view onto it.

The lock's open and close pings define when a ride starts and ends — not the app's buttons. Every entry point is therefore resumable, and a dead phone can't run a fare. This is the single most load-bearing choice in the architecture.

Where decision 03 came from

It wasn't a principle I started with. It came out of one edge case — what happens if the rider's phone dies mid-ride — which exposed an assumption buried in my first model: that the app was the source of truth for when a ride ends. It isn't, and it can't be. If the app owns ride state, a dead battery means a fare running until the bike's does.

Moving authority to the lock pings fixed that, and then paid for itself three more times: it is why F12 needs no on-device screen, why L4 can expire a session mid-ride without interrupting anything, and why going offline at A11 is survivable — the rider locks the bike and walks away, and the receipt catches up when the phone reconnects.

01How to read this

STEP a required node on the spine STATE a variant of a step, not its own destination BRANCH a failure node with a defined return path LAYER a condition that can apply at many steps SURFACE a persistent destination outside the funnel SILENT agent work with no screen at all

The distinction between branch and layer is the one that does the most work here. A branch is somewhere the rider lands; it has a screen and an exit. A layer is a condition — offline, permission denied, session expired — that changes how several steps behave without being a place you can be. Treating connectivity as a branch is how apps end up with a dead-end error screen at every step.

02Launch routing — the question asked before anything renders

Because ride state is server-side, a cold start can't assume it is starting at the beginning. It resolves identity, then an active ride, then an active hold — in that order — and routes past the funnel entirely if either exists. The rider who force-quits mid-ride and reopens the app lands back on her ride, not on a map.

03The map — twelve steps and everything that hangs off them

The spine is linear on purpose. The only genuine fork in the whole product is at A5, where a rider without a destination can fall back to a floor-only browse — and that is labelled as the weaker guarantee it is, not offered as an equal path.

Branches sit to the right of the step that produces them. Every one has a stated return path; none is a dead end, and none requires the rider to start over.

05Ride state machine — and who is allowed to change it

The screen inventory describes what the rider is looking at. This describes what is true, which is a different thing and the one that money depends on. Two transitions in here cannot be made by the app at all: the bike has to agree.

The pattern worth noticing across the table

Six of the twelve branches cost the rider nothing at all, and the remaining six cost her a walk or a re-pick. None of them cost her money she didn't agree to. That is not twelve separate acts of generosity — it falls out of two structural choices made once: the fare only accrues in riding and paused, and it stops on the failure event rather than when the ride formally closes.

Get those two right and the kind interface writes itself. Get them wrong and no amount of apologetic microcopy saves it.

07Cross-cutting layers

Conditions, not places. Each one changes how several steps behave without ever becoming a screen the rider is stuck on — which is the trap: treat connectivity as a branch and you end up with a dead-end error page at every step of the funnel.

L1Location permission

Denied is a degraded state, not a blocked one — and barely degraded at that.

A5
Unaffected. A destination is typed, not sensed.
A6
Falls back to manual area search. Readiness still works — it needs the destination and the bikes' positions, not hers.
A7
Walk time and distance degrade to approximate. Range framing is untouched.
A11
Zone confirmation uses the bike's GPS, so ending a ride is unaffected.

L2Camera permission

Barely a layer at all, because the alternative was never a fallback.

A9
Manual bike-ID entry sits beside the scanner at equal weight, permission or not. QR scanning needs visual targeting and steady motor control not every rider has.
Everywhere
No blocked screen exists anywhere in the product for a denied camera.

L3Connectivity

The phase you lose signal in decides what happens — and the back half is survivable because the bike holds the truth.

A1–A4
Blocked with retry. Nothing to cache; no account exists yet.
A5–A6
Last-known map with an honest banner, and reservation disabled — readiness can't be verified, so the product won't pretend.
A8–A9
Can't authorise or confirm a lock → F2 path. Nothing charged.
A10
Ride continues; the bike holds lock state. "Reconnecting…", fare accrues server-side, reconciled on return.
A11
Lock it and walk away. The close ping ends the ride; the receipt catches up.
A12
Queued. Appears in G3 when the phone reconnects.

L4Session expiry

Re-auth returns her to the node she was on — never dumped to home, and never mid-ride.

A1–A9
Re-auth, then resume at the same step with form state intact.
A10
The ride is untouched. It lives on the server; expiry is a viewing problem, not a riding problem. Re-auth lands on E2 → A10.
A11–A12
Lock-close still ends the ride. The receipt waits in G3.

08Persistent surfaces

Everything outside the ride funnel. All five hang off A5/A6 only — never reachable from A7 onward, which is Decision 02 enforced structurally rather than by hiding a tab bar. The single exception is the ride screen's "Report an issue", and that returns to the ride rather than leaving it.

09What this architecture deliberately doesn't have

An IA is defined as much by its omissions, and each of these was a live option that got rejected for a reason.

  • No bottom tab bar. The ride is a task, not a hierarchy. Tabs would invite the rider out of a funnel holding a payment authorisation and a reserved bike.
  • No onboarding carousel. The design target is a returning category user who rides three or four times a day. Slides are friction for her — a deliberate trade against the first-time rider, and the one most worth testing.
  • No in-app assistant or chat surface. This is the product thesis, not an omission of convenience: a conversational layer is a well-designed apology for a failure that should have been prevented.
  • No confidence score anywhere in the rider app. Readiness, battery decay and billing all produce confidence internally. None of it is shown. A bike that is confident doesn't justify itself with a number — it just appears.
  • No browse-without-a-destination as a first-class root. It exists as a labelled fallback on the 2 km floor, with a banner saying exactly that. The product never pretends a floor-based check is a trip-based one.
  • No rating gate. The star rating on A12 is skippable. Trapping a rider behind a rating to see her own receipt would undo the screen it sits on.
  • No dead ends. Every one of the twelve branches names an exit. If a node has no onward path, it isn't finished.
The one I'd still argue about

Hiding bikes is a strong move with an obvious failure mode: an empty map can read as a broken app rather than a system exercising care. Absence is paid for in two places — F1 naming the check out loud, and A6 narrating removals when the destination changes — but whether that reads as diligence to a first-time rider is untested, and she is exactly the person with no prior trust to draw on.

Hide rate and F1 frequency are the two numbers that would settle it. They're instrumented for that reason, and they're the dial that would move the 1.5× multiplier if it turned out I'd set it too conservatively.
